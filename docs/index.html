GameCore

A living-room box for the consoles people actually want to emulate.

PS3, PS4, Switch, Wii U and Xbox 360 — plus eight more systems — driven entirely with a gamepad, on an Arch Linux machine that stays a real computer underneath.

Three things it does differently

Most living-room emulation systems are built around decades of older machines, shipped as a frozen image. This one takes the opposite bet.

The recent consoles come first

Thirteen systems, weighted towards PS3, PS4, Switch, Wii U and Xbox 360 — not thirty 8-bit machines with the modern emulators bolted on at the end of the list.

Emulators that stay current

They are Flatpaks from Flathub and update on their own schedule. RPCS3 and Ryujinx change every few weeks; on a frozen system image a compatibility fix waits for the image's next release.

Still a real machine

A full Arch install with a KDE Plasma desktop underneath. Nothing is read-only, nothing is locked. One command closes the kiosk and hands the computer back.

Plug a controller in. That's the configuration step.

GameCore writes each emulator's own config for the pads actually connected, per player slot. The first pad plugged in is Player 1 whatever brand it is, the next is Player 2 — like a real console. No player slot is ever tied to a brand.

This is harder than it sounds, and it is where most of the work went. Every emulator identifies devices differently: some speak SDL's role vocabulary, some match a literal device name whose numbering is not the player slot, and one binds by a GUID that differs between USB and Bluetooth for the same pad — and fails silently when it does not match.

For a controller nothing recognises, a built-in wizard maps it in about a minute: one button at a time, full screen, driven entirely by the pad being mapped. Press to record, hold to skip a button the pad does not have, double-press to go back. No keyboard at any point.

Installing it

On a machine already running Arch Linux or Manjaro, a graphical installer does the whole job — emulators, services, auto-login and the kiosk session. 

chmod +x gamecore-installer
./gamecore-installer

Pick your emulators, hit Install. It asks for the administrator password once, through polkit. Re-running it is safe. After that the box updates itself over the air from the settings screen.

There is a command-line path too, for a machine you only reach over SSH — see the README.

What it does not do

Better read here than found out after an install.

  • Arch and Manjaro only. The installer is specific to them. If you run something else, this is not for you today.
  • X11 only. The overlay system, the fullscreen enforcer and the gamepad-to-keyboard bridge all depend on it. Wayland is not a small change.
  • The first install needs a network, because the emulators come from Flathub.
  • Multi-controller setups are proven by a test harness, not by hardware. Two-, three- and four-pad configurations are replayed as synthetic controller sets against the real config generators and compared against recorded output. That is real evidence for the generated configs. It is not four people playing on a real box.
  • One developer. The bus factor is one.
  • No games, ROMs, BIOS files or keys are distributed, downloaded or included. Bring your own.
